# Business operating model — the system beyond the code

Raven + Shadow must become a **service a company can responsibly purchase**, not just an agent network. All figures and market directions below are hypotheses until validated through customer discovery.

## The first paying problem

**Hypothesis:** Teams adopting AI in operational workflows struggle to know whether reported AI completion represents the actual result. Example: a support agent says it resolved a ticket, but the required backend state was not changed by the deadline.

**Proposed initial offer: Shadow Assurance Assessment**

- **Scope:** One outcome contract, one authorized operational workflow, a limited source/observation package, independent comparison and reviewed report.
- **Buyer:** Engineering operations, automation leads, customer operations, or leaders accountable for a specific AI-enabled process.
- **Deliverable:** What the workflow was intended to do; what it claimed; what was independently observed; traceable evidence; verdict/unknowns; priority fixes and next verification step.
- **Value proof:** False-positive success detections, preventable incidents, time to investigate, reproducibility and reduction in repeated errors.
- **Pricing:** Not validated. Conduct buyer interviews before committing to assessment fees or subscription pricing.

## Commercial capabilities the final company requires

| Function | Must exist before taking payment or confidential material | Later scalable operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positioning and sales | Clear target buyer, narrowly defined claim, qualification questions, scope and proposal | Repeatable case studies, acquisition channels and renewal strategy |
| Legal and permission | Approved scope/authorization, customer agreement, confidentiality and service limitations reviewed by counsel as appropriate | Negotiated enterprise data protection, procurement and service commitments |
| Privacy and security | Data classification, access controls, retention/deletion policy, incident channel, key management and secure transfer | Security evidence program, audits and enterprise procurement controls |
| Delivery and quality | Human-reviewed proof, explicit unknowns, reproducible evidence record and agreed acceptance | Self-service customer portal, monitoring and customer success |
| Operations | Support/contact process, incident and backup playbooks, availability assumptions, billing/accounting | Service levels, staffing, margins and capacity planning |
| Engineering economics | Measure model usage, infrastructure expense, review labor, failure rate and time saved | Unit economics and pricing based on observed outcomes |

## Founder and customer division of responsibility

- **Founder/operator:** Define scope, ensure permissions, choose/approve any consequential live action, conduct reviews and customer communication, manage contracts and operations.
- **Raven/Queen:** Automate bounded investigation, evidence gathering and coordination within the granted scope; surface conflicts and unknowns.
- **Shadow/Quality:** Independently verify assertions and release conditions; block unsupported completion claims.
- **Customer:** Authorize system access and data boundaries; determine production-change authority; confirm business priorities; review results within the engagement contract.

## Expansion sequence

Begin with a narrow human-reviewed assessment. Once verified and repeatable, offer monitoring for the same proof class. Only then add policy-authorized reversible remediation and broader enterprise connectors. Avoid claiming the product works for every industry or every arbitrary problem in its first release.
