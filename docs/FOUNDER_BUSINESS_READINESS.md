# Founder business readiness — public prototype boundary

**This is a public product/operations assessment, not a private-engine audit or
permission to accept customer data.** It starts from public prototype baseline
`2321901` and the complete public blueprint, operator specification, requirements,
roadmap, demonstration guide and business model. The private repository, native
runtime, services, source snapshot and qualification evidence are outside scope.

## Evidence and status rules

Three different kinds of statement must remain separate:

1. **Verified public presentation behavior.** The existing public receipts establish
   the fictional source viewer, citations, four observation variants, synthetic
   timeline/colony projection and internal report export. Their earlier results
   were 12 deterministic checks, 37 local browser checks and seven hosted smoke
   checks. The founder candidate separately passes 34 unit, 37 founder browser and
   37 existing browser regression checks, with
   [final receipts](../tests/founder/acceptance-manifest.json). These verify the
   public presentation; they do not qualify a customer engagement.
2. **Historical public claims about private engineering.** The dated record in
   [CURRENT_STATE.md](CURRENT_STATE.md#historical-engineering-record--published-2026-10-29)
   describes local source snapshots, intake permissions and duplicate-safe recovery,
   with historical tests. The blueprint also describes native Store, Queen, worker,
   model-routing, Shadow, Quality and Archive responsibilities. This campaign does
   not reverify their current implementation, availability or behavior. Architecture
   intent is not evidence that an integration is operating.
3. **Pilot gates not qualified by this public evidence.** A public sample, a valid
   source digest or a usable screen does not establish customer permission,
   independent authentication, real-model investigation quality, accepted review,
   secure delivery, incident readiness or a commercially repeatable service.

The actual [founder cockpit audit](FOUNDER_COCKPIT_AUDIT.md) links the immutable
A–H walkthrough, controlled recovery/keyboard failures and selected improvements.
Its initial evidence supports five usability groups, three deliberate future
integration gaps and two reproducible presentation defects. Final evidence also
covers three preparation/export/cancel recovery cases discovered during review.
All observed presentation defects are addressed; future native and operating
capabilities remain unqualified by this public evidence. This capability and
dependency assessment does not substitute documentary inspection for that browser
evidence or qualify the private engine.

## Founder scenarios and the boundary of today's demonstration

| Scenario | What the public baseline demonstrates | What the founder still needs for a real engagement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Start the day | One fictional Acme case, visible proof labels and a deterministic result after a check | A tenant-scoped engagement register; states, blockers, accountable owner and next permitted action from current canonical records |
| B — Onboard a company | Load a built-in fictional source set and inspect its demonstration scope | Customer identity, signed scope, authority/expiry, data classification, safe transfer, retention and revocation; a sample-selection click is not customer consent |
| C — Direct an investigation | A fixed Acme question/contract and illustrative Queen/worker timeline | Versioned question, expected state, deadline, constraints and budget; actual authorized task assignments, nested worker lineage, peer handoffs and recovery state |
| D — Investigate evidence | Exact sample source text, citation lines and digest identity | Authorized original retrieval, immutable versions, freshness, provenance and access enforcement across real sources; hashes alone cannot establish truth |
| E — Use Shadow | Separate deterministic comparison with supplied fictional observations; PASS, FAIL and NOT ASSESSED examples | Independently authenticated observation and attributable verifier/proof class; separate investigation, Quality, Queen and human-release states |
| F — Respond to a problem | Contradicted, absent and stale fictional observations; explicit unknowns | Reasoned blocker/owner/next action, authorized requests for evidence, safe bounded recovery and escalation with no invented success |
| G — Review a deliverable | Inspect and download an internal, unapproved fictional report | Complete current-artifact review, distinct Quality/Queen/human decisions, secure scoped delivery, version binding and revocation |
| H — Supervise the company | Presentation status and architecture/roadmap disclosures | Real engagement queue, accountable decisions, worker reliability, measured cost, operational alerts, customer follow-up, support and billing records |

A useful cockpit should make the sequence understandable without implying that
all steps are available: **scope → evidence → investigation → outcome comparison
→ review → human release → follow-up**. An eligible next step is distinct from an
approval, and a successful fictional comparison is distinct from a completed
customer investigation.

## Top three blockers to a supervised pilot

1. **Authority and safe handling.** Establish one customer's written scope, verified
   identities, least-privilege source access, isolation, retention and revocation
   before receiving confidential material. This is a prerequisite for authentic
   observation and investigation, not a later visual improvement.
2. **A trustworthy end-to-end investigation.** Qualify real reasoning and source
   navigation through the existing native contracts, together with an independently
   authenticated observation. Preserve unknowns, failure evidence, supported
   calculations and all original requirements. A supplied fictional observation
   cannot meet this gate.
3. **Accountable review, release and service operation.** Bind Quality, Queen and
   human release to the same current report/evidence version, then demonstrate safe
   delivery, a named support/incident owner, recoverable records and measured cost.
   A download button cannot stand in for those controls.

These are ranked by business dependency and potential harm. They are not newly
verified defects in private code, and they are not authority to build a public
customer backend.

## Minimum pilot capabilities, dependencies and smallest useful step

| Priority / gate | Required outcome | Dependencies | Smallest useful future integration step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P0 pilot blocker — client scope and onboarding | One defined question, outcome, permitted systems/actions, deadline, exclusions, contacts, retention and written authorization | Agreed offer and accountable founder/customer roles | Display one authenticated, versioned engagement/scope record read-only; refuse to imply consent from source content or UI state |
| P0 pilot blocker — identity and source permissions | Verified organization/principal, least privilege, tenant separation, grant expiry/revocation and protected originals | Scope and identity authority | Project a server-evaluated capability and source list from the canonical intake boundary; qualify cross-tenant, revoked and expired access before enabling real intake |
| P0 pilot blocker — independent observations | Known observer identity, authorized system, observation time, coverage/freshness and immutable captured evidence | Authorized access, outcome contract and protected storage | Add one read-only authenticated observer for a narrowly defined proof class, with missing/stale/contradictory negatives and explicit NOT ASSESSED behavior |
| P1 pilot blocker — reliable real-model investigation | Genuine source-linked reasoning, reproducible numerical relationships, bounded tasks and honest failure/recovery | Canonical source scope, existing model router and worker contracts | Expose one independently qualified native investigation snapshot with actual task/event references; do not create a browser scheduler or fabricate a successful sample as proof |
| P0 pilot blocker — review and human release | Separate Quality, Queen and human decisions apply to the current complete artifact set and permissions | Completed investigation, observation evidence and integrity checks | Render linked review/release records and why a gate is locked; only a future authorized server action may change their state |
| P0 pilot blocker — secure delivery | Correct recipient, authenticated access, scoped current report, expiry/revocation and delivery audit | Identity, accepted reviews and exact human release | Qualify one permission-checked report delivery path; keep public demo exports fictional and unapproved |
| P0 pilot blocker — monitoring, recovery and incident response | Named operator, meaningful alerts, backup/restore drill, incident contact, retention and operational limits | Canonical event/evidence records and deployment boundaries | Present actual stale/error/connectivity indicators and a tested recovery/incident runbook; show unavailable telemetry as unavailable, never as zero incidents or perfect uptime |
| P1 pilot blocker — cost and capacity | Recorded model/worker usage, review labor and expense limits support a repeatable engagement | Attributable runtime telemetry and pricing inputs | Read measured usage/budget records for one engagement; show unknown cost as unknown and enforce spending authority outside the browser |
| P1 pilot blocker — follow-up, billing and support | Defined next observation/action, accountable contact, customer follow-up, invoice/support process and service terms | Accepted scope, delivery, customer agreement and operating ownership | Link one founder-owned follow-up record to the engagement and report; a documented manual supervised process may precede a full CRM or billing integration |

P0/P1 here identify **pilot release priorities**, not security vulnerabilities found
in this static demonstration. Later automation may improve scale, but a supervised
pilot still needs an explicit, accountable process for every release-critical gate.

## Minimum future read-projection contract

The eventual UI should consume a typed, permission-filtered projection from the
existing canonical system. This is a design contract only: no endpoint, database,
credential exchange or backend implementation is introduced in this campaign.

| Projection field / group | Minimum meaning and refusal behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `identity` | Opaque tenant, engagement, mission and version IDs; authenticated principal/scope reference and server-evaluated audience/capabilities. Names or source prose grant no authority. |
| `provenance` | Explicit origin, producer, record/event references, captured/observed timestamps and integrity status, plus a separate proof class: synthetic example, recorded event, unverified model claim, independently checked outcome or future functionality. Canonical storage alone proves neither truth nor independent verification; replay requires actual event records. Missing provenance cannot be promoted by UI labels. |
| `scope` | Versioned investigation question, expected-state contract, evidence deadline, permitted source references/actions, expiry, constraints, budget and accountable owner. A changed scope invalidates dependent eligibility until reevaluated. |
| `lifecycle` | Typed current engagement/mission stage, blocker reasons, owner and next permitted action references. Queued, active, blocked, completed, reviewed and released remain distinguishable. |
| `sources` | Immutable original IDs/version hashes, classification, permitted excerpts, exact line or offset references, freshness and authorization state. Citation retrieval must remain tenant/scope checked. |
| `investigation` | Actual task snapshots, completion/coverage status, unknowns, failures and evidence references. No status is inferred from the presence of a report file or a model's success claim. |
| `workers` | Parent/child lineage, scoped role, actual task ownership, ordered event/message IDs, acknowledgments, attempt/recovery state and resource limits. The graph projects these records; it does not own them. |
| `outcome` | PASS / FAIL / NOT ASSESSED for a specific contract and observation, with reasons, observation identity/freshness, verifier identity/version and proof class. The result is separate from source integrity and investigation completeness. |
| `reviews` | Separate Quality and Queen records with verdict, reviewer identity, time, artifact/evidence snapshot hashes and their linked dependency. Missing, superseded or mismatched records do not approve current work. |
| `release` | Locked/eligible/approved/rejected/revoked state, explicit human authority, scope, current artifact hashes and decision record. UI eligibility is not approval; approval is not automatically delivery. |
| `operations` | Actual connection/staleness, measured request/usage/cost/budget data, alerts and follow-up/support references with owners. Distinguish unavailable (`null`/typed unknown) from verified zero. |

A future snapshot should also carry a schema version, monotonically ordered
revision/event cursor, observation time and stale-state policy. Responses must be
coherent for one engagement/version; late responses cannot resurrect revoked
access, old report eligibility or a superseded result. Missing fields render
unknown/unavailable rather than becoming positive claims. UI failure must not
mutate durable work or permissions.

An unexecuted comparison is NOT RUN, separate from a computed NOT ASSESSED result.
A business FAIL can be the valid conclusion of an accepted investigation; it must
not automatically be treated as failed Quality or denied human release. Conversely,
a business PASS grants neither review acceptance nor release authority.

Any future command interface is separate from this read projection: it must
reauthorize at the canonical boundary, bind the expected current version, use an
idempotency key where applicable, and return an attributable outcome/receipt.
Receiving a UI click, model proposal or a cached capability is not proof that an
external action succeeded. No such command endpoint is implemented here.

## Requirements preserved

Raven remains the founder-facing narrator and eventual top-fed contextual
conversation. Queen remains the canonical coordinator of scoped, recursively
expanding specialists and their attributable peer communication; there is no new
arbitrary worker-population ceiling. Shadow remains independently attributable;
Quality, Queen acceptance and human release remain distinct. Provider-independent
shared context and the existing local model-router policy are preserved.

Reviewed self-optimization, safe recovery, Archive protection of founder/customer
originals, Queen-approved cleanup of identified generated artifacts, consent-based
voice/SMS, and the crown-free event-backed neural view remain in the cumulative
requirements. Their deferral from this public cockpit does not remove them.
A synthetic explanation may teach the process, but cannot impersonate real Raven
inference or actual worker messages.
